## Decisions and limits carried forward

The lock serializes the corpus, which is stronger and simpler than per-item
locking but reduces unrelated-write throughput. It requires a local filesystem.
External editors/bare file tools do not hold the lock: detected conflicts are
preserved, but a check-to-rename race or edit after final verification cannot be
made atomic. A multi-item command can commit a prefix before a later conflict.
Recovery does not automatically three-way merge divergent text; conflict journals
remain durable evidence for a fresh-base write or a new staged proposal.

Back up `.arbiter/transactions/`: it is canonical history, not index cache.
Full snapshots grow over time; retention/compaction is deferred. Disk errors or
corrupt journals stop the operation; never remove history to claim success.
Old cleaned receipts attest an earlier commit, so replay suppresses their effects
rather than overwriting newer intent. Propose emits one op by default; authors may
edit the pending ops list before its first arbitration, then keep its ID immutable.

T05 must keep its future replaceable current checkpoint separate from these
retained receipts. T03 retains responsibility for timestamp-only invalidation,
visibility policy and nonterminal needs-review absorption; no projection fix was
implemented here. Bootstrap guidance advances explicitly; existing installed
protocols are not silently rewritten. Downgrade writers do not receive the new
cooperating-writer guarantee.
